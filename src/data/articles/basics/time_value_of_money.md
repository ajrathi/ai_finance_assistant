## The Core Concept

A dollar today is worth more than a dollar in the future. Why? Because money available now can be invested to earn returns, while future money hasn't had that opportunity.

This principle — the **time value of money (TVM)** — underpins virtually all financial decisions.

## Present Value and Future Value

**Future Value (FV)**: What a sum today will be worth later, given a return rate.
- FV = PV × (1 + r)^n
- $1,000 today at 6% for 5 years = $1,338

**Present Value (PV)**: What a future sum is worth in today's dollars.
- PV = FV / (1 + r)^n
- $1,338 in 5 years, discounted at 6% = $1,000 today

## Discount Rate

The discount rate is the interest rate used to convert future values to present values. It represents the "cost" of waiting, or the opportunity cost of not having the money now.

A higher discount rate makes future money worth less today. A lottery jackpot of $1M in 20 years is worth far less than $1M today.

## Why This Matters in Financial Decisions

### Evaluating Investments
Would you rather have $500 today or $600 in 3 years? If you could earn 6% invested, $500 × (1.06)^3 = $595. The $600 future payment (barely) wins.

### Loan Comparisons
A 30-year mortgage vs. 15-year: TVM helps you see the true total cost of each option, including opportunity cost.

### Retirement Planning
Contributions made at 25 vs. 45 have vastly different future values due to compounding time.

## Annuities

An annuity is a series of equal payments over time.

**Future Value of Annuity** (what your regular investments become):
- $500/month for 30 years at 7% = ~$567,000

**Present Value of Annuity** (what a stream of future payments is worth today):
- Used to value pension payments, bond coupons, etc.

## Practical Applications

1. **Mortgage choice**: 15-year vs. 30-year — which costs more in total interest?
2. **Lease vs. buy**: What's the present value of all lease payments vs. purchase price?
3. **Saving for college**: How much to save monthly to reach $100,000 in 18 years?
4. **Evaluating a raise**: Is a $5,000 signing bonus better than a $2,000 annual raise?

## Key Takeaways

1. Money now is worth more than money later
2. Future Value = Present Value × (1 + r)^n
3. Present Value = Future Value / (1 + r)^n
4. Higher discount rates make future money worth less today
