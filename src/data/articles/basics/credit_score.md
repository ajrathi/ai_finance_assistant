## What is a Credit Score?

A credit score is a 3-digit number (typically 300–850) that lenders use to assess how likely you are to repay debt. The higher your score, the more creditworthy you appear — and the better loan terms you'll qualify for.

The most widely used scoring model is **FICO**, developed by Fair Isaac Corporation.

## Score Ranges

| Score | Category | Impact |
|-------|----------|--------|
| 800–850 | Exceptional | Best rates available |
| 740–799 | Very Good | Near-best rates |
| 670–739 | Good | Most lenders approve |
| 580–669 | Fair | Higher rates, some rejections |
| 300–579 | Poor | Limited access, very high rates |

## The Five Factors (FICO)

### 1. Payment History (35%)
The biggest factor. Do you pay on time? Even one 30-day late payment can drop your score significantly.

### 2. Amounts Owed / Credit Utilization (30%)
How much of your available credit are you using? Keep utilization below 30% (ideally under 10%).
- $1,000 balance on a $10,000 limit = 10% utilization (excellent)
- $4,500 balance on a $5,000 limit = 90% utilization (damages score)

### 3. Length of Credit History (15%)
Older accounts help your score. Avoid closing old credit cards — they shorten your average account age.

### 4. Credit Mix (10%)
Having different types of credit (credit cards, auto loan, mortgage) shows you can manage various debt types.

### 5. New Credit / Hard Inquiries (10%)
Each credit application triggers a "hard inquiry," which temporarily lowers your score slightly. Multiple applications in a short period raise flags.

## How to Improve Your Score

1. **Pay every bill on time** — set up autopay for at least minimum payments
2. **Lower credit card balances** — pay down debt to reduce utilization
3. **Don't close old accounts** — keep them open even if unused
4. **Limit new credit applications** — only apply when needed
5. **Check your credit report** — dispute any errors (free at AnnualCreditReport.com)

## Credit Report vs. Credit Score

- **Credit report**: Detailed record of your credit history (accounts, payment history, inquiries)
- **Credit score**: Numerical summary derived from your credit report

You're entitled to one free credit report from each of the three bureaus (Equifax, Experian, TransUnion) annually.

## Why Your Score Matters Beyond Loans

Your credit score affects:
- Mortgage and auto loan interest rates (potentially saving tens of thousands)
- Apartment rental applications
- Insurance premiums in some states
- Employment background checks (some employers review credit)

## Key Takeaways

1. Payment history (35%) is the most important factor
2. Keep credit utilization below 30%
3. Don't close old accounts unnecessarily
4. Check your credit report annually for errors

*For educational purposes only. Credit scoring models and criteria vary by lender.*
