## What is Financial Planning?

Financial planning is the process of setting goals, assessing your current situation, and creating a strategy to achieve those goals. It's your personalized roadmap from where you are financially to where you want to be.

Good financial planning isn't about being rich — it's about making intentional decisions with your money so that your financial life supports the life you want to live.

## The Financial Planning Process

### Step 1: Know Where You Stand
Calculate your:
- **Net worth**: Assets − Liabilities
- **Monthly cash flow**: Income − Expenses
- **Debt obligations**: All debts, rates, and balances

You can't plan a journey without knowing your starting point.

### Step 2: Define Your Goals
Goals should be **SMART**:
- **S**pecific: "Save $20,000 for a down payment"
- **M**easurable: Track progress monthly
- **A**chievable: Realistic given your income and expenses
- **R**elevant: Aligned with your values
- **T**ime-bound: "By December 2026"

**Common financial goals:**
- Build emergency fund (3–6 months of expenses)
- Pay off high-interest debt
- Save for house down payment
- Retire comfortably
- Fund children's education
- Achieve financial independence

### Step 3: Create a Budget
A budget is the mechanism that moves money from "spending" to "goals." The 50/30/20 rule or zero-based budgeting both work — choose what you'll actually use.

### Step 4: Build Your Foundation
Before investing or big goals:
1. Emergency fund (1,000 starter → 3–6 months)
2. High-interest debt eliminated
3. Adequate insurance coverage

### Step 5: Grow Wealth
Once the foundation is solid:
1. Maximize employer 401(k) match
2. Contribute to Roth IRA
3. Maximize 401(k)
4. Invest in taxable brokerage
5. Consider real estate

### Step 6: Protect and Review
- Review financial plan annually
- Update as life changes (marriage, children, job change, inheritance)
- Ensure insurance coverage is adequate (life, disability, health, property)

## The Financial Priority Order

1. **Emergency fund** ($1,000 starter)
2. **Employer 401(k) match** (free money)
3. **High-interest debt payoff** (>7% interest)
4. **Emergency fund** (3–6 months full)
5. **Roth IRA maximum** ($7,000/year)
6. **401(k) maximum** ($23,000/year)
7. **Additional investing** (taxable brokerage, real estate)
8. **Low-interest debt** (student loans, mortgage at <4-5%)

## Common Financial Planning Mistakes

**Living paycheck to paycheck**: No buffer for unexpected expenses. Solution: emergency fund.

**No retirement savings in your 20s**: Compound growth requires time. Even small early contributions dwarf larger late contributions.

**Lifestyle inflation**: Spending increases with every raise, leaving nothing extra. Solution: automate savings increases with each raise.

**Ignoring insurance**: One catastrophic illness, accident, or lawsuit can wipe out years of savings. Solution: adequate health, disability, and liability insurance.

**No will or estate plan**: Your assets may not go where you intend. Update beneficiary designations on accounts.

**Paying only minimums on credit cards**: At 20% APR, a $5,000 balance takes over 30 years to pay off with minimums.

## Financial Independence vs. Retirement

**Financial independence**: Having enough invested wealth to cover your living expenses indefinitely from investment returns — optional work.

**The 25x Rule / FI Number**: Your annual expenses × 25 = the portfolio size needed for financial independence (based on the 4% safe withdrawal rate).
- Spend $60,000/year: Need $1,500,000 invested
- Spend $40,000/year: Need $1,000,000 invested

## When to Seek Professional Help

Consider a financial advisor for:
- Complex tax situations
- Estate planning
- Major life transitions (divorce, inheritance, retirement)
- Business ownership
- Specialized investment needs

Look for a **fee-only fiduciary** — they're legally required to act in your best interest and don't earn commissions from products they recommend.

## Key Takeaways

1. Financial planning = knowing where you are + setting goals + creating a strategy
2. Build the foundation first: emergency fund, high-interest debt, employer match
3. SMART goals are Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Relevant, Time-bound
4. Automate savings to remove temptation and build discipline
5. Review and update your plan annually or after major life changes

*For educational purposes only. Personal financial situations vary. Consider working with a fee-only financial advisor for complex planning needs.*
