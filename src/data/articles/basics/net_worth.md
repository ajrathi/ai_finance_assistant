## What is Net Worth?

Net worth is the most fundamental measure of your financial health. It answers the question: "If I sold everything I owned and paid off all my debts, how much would I have left?"

**Net Worth = Total Assets − Total Liabilities**

## Assets: What You Own

Assets are things of value that you own:

**Liquid assets** (easily convertible to cash):
- Checking and savings accounts
- Money market accounts
- Cash and cash equivalents

**Investment assets**:
- Stocks, bonds, ETFs
- Retirement accounts (401k, IRA)
- Brokerage accounts

**Physical assets**:
- Home (market value)
- Vehicles
- Jewelry, collectibles, business interests

## Liabilities: What You Owe

Liabilities are all outstanding debts:

**Short-term liabilities:**
- Credit card balances
- Medical bills
- Personal loans

**Long-term liabilities:**
- Mortgage balance
- Auto loans
- Student loans

## Calculating Your Net Worth: Example

**Assets:**
- Checking account: $3,000
- Savings account: $12,000
- 401(k): $45,000
- Home value: $350,000
- Car: $18,000
- **Total Assets: $428,000**

**Liabilities:**
- Credit cards: $4,500
- Car loan: $12,000
- Mortgage balance: $280,000
- Student loans: $25,000
- **Total Liabilities: $321,500**

**Net Worth: $428,000 − $321,500 = $106,500**

## Is Your Net Worth Good?

Net worth benchmarks vary by age. A common guideline (from "The Millionaire Next Door"):

**By age:**
- 30: ~$75,000
- 40: ~$200,000
- 50: ~$400,000
- 60: ~$800,000

However, these are averages — your circumstances matter. What matters most is that your net worth is **growing over time**.

## How to Increase Your Net Worth

1. **Increase income**: Salary raises, side income, investments
2. **Reduce liabilities**: Pay down high-interest debt first
3. **Grow investments**: Regular contributions to retirement and brokerage accounts
4. **Avoid depreciating assets**: Cars, electronics lose value quickly
5. **Build home equity**: Mortgage payments build ownership stake

## Tracking Net Worth

Review your net worth quarterly using a simple spreadsheet or apps. Tracking creates accountability and motivation. A rising net worth — even slowly — is a sign you're moving in the right direction.

## Common Misconceptions

**"High income = high net worth"** — False. Many high earners have low net worth due to spending or debt.

**"I need to own a home"** — Home equity is an asset, but renting while investing the difference can also build net worth.

**"My net worth is negative — I'm doomed"** — Many people start with negative net worth (especially student loan debt). What matters is the direction of change.
