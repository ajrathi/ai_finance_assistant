## What is Compound Interest?

Compound interest is often called the "eighth wonder of the world." It is interest calculated on both your initial principal AND the interest you've already earned. This creates a snowball effect where your money grows faster and faster over time.

## Simple vs. Compound Interest

**Simple interest** is calculated only on the principal:
- You deposit $1,000 at 5% simple interest
- After 10 years: $1,000 + ($1,000 × 5% × 10) = $1,500

**Compound interest** earns interest on interest:
- You deposit $1,000 at 5% compounded annually
- After 10 years: $1,000 × (1.05)^10 = $1,629

The difference ($129) seems small at first, but grows dramatically over longer periods.

## The Formula

A = P × (1 + r/n)^(n×t)

Where:
- **A** = final amount
- **P** = principal (starting amount)
- **r** = annual interest rate (as a decimal, e.g., 5% = 0.05)
- **n** = number of times interest compounds per year
- **t** = time in years

## Compounding Frequency

Interest can compound at different frequencies:
- **Annually**: Once per year
- **Quarterly**: 4 times per year
- **Monthly**: 12 times per year
- **Daily**: 365 times per year

More frequent compounding means slightly more growth. $10,000 at 5% for 10 years:
- Annual compounding: $16,289
- Monthly compounding: $16,470
- Daily compounding: $16,487

## The Rule of 72

A useful shortcut: divide 72 by the annual interest rate to estimate how many years it takes to double your money.
- At 6% annual return: 72 ÷ 6 = **12 years to double**
- At 9% annual return: 72 ÷ 9 = **8 years to double**

## Why Starting Early Matters

The biggest factor in compound growth is time. Consider two investors:
- **Alex** invests $5,000/year from age 25–35 (10 years, $50,000 total), then stops
- **Jordan** invests $5,000/year from age 35–65 (30 years, $150,000 total)

Assuming 7% annual return, at age 65:
- Alex has approximately **$602,000**
- Jordan has approximately **$472,000**

Alex invested LESS money but ended up with MORE — because of a 10-year head start.

## Compound Interest Works Against You Too

Credit card debt uses compound interest against you. If you carry a $5,000 balance at 20% APR and make only minimum payments, it can take decades to pay off and cost thousands in interest.

## Key Takeaways

1. Compound interest accelerates wealth growth over time
2. Starting early is more powerful than investing more later
3. The Rule of 72 helps estimate doubling time
4. High-interest debt compounds against you — pay it off quickly
