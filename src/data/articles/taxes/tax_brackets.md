## The Progressive Tax System

The U.S. uses a **progressive tax system** — you pay higher rates only on income above certain thresholds. This is often misunderstood: being in the 24% tax bracket does NOT mean you pay 24% on all your income.

## 2024 Federal Tax Brackets (Single Filers)

| Tax Rate | Income Range |
|----------|--------------|
| 10% | $0 – $11,600 |
| 12% | $11,601 – $47,150 |
| 22% | $47,151 – $100,525 |
| 24% | $100,526 – $191,950 |
| 32% | $191,951 – $243,725 |
| 35% | $243,726 – $609,350 |
| 37% | Over $609,350 |

## 2024 Federal Tax Brackets (Married Filing Jointly)

| Tax Rate | Income Range |
|----------|--------------|
| 10% | $0 – $23,200 |
| 12% | $23,201 – $94,300 |
| 22% | $94,301 – $201,050 |
| 24% | $201,051 – $383,900 |
| 32% | $383,901 – $487,450 |
| 35% | $487,451 – $731,200 |
| 37% | Over $731,200 |

## Marginal vs. Effective Tax Rate

**Marginal Tax Rate**: The rate applied to your last dollar of income (the bracket you're in).
**Effective Tax Rate**: Your actual average rate — total taxes paid ÷ total income.

### Example
A single filer with $75,000 taxable income:
- First $11,600 × 10% = $1,160
- Next $35,550 ($47,150 − $11,600) × 12% = $4,266
- Remaining $27,850 ($75,000 − $47,150) × 22% = $6,127
- **Total tax: $11,553**
- **Effective rate: $11,553 ÷ $75,000 = 15.4%**

Even though they're in the 22% bracket, they pay an effective rate of just 15.4%.

## Standard Deduction (2024)

Before calculating your taxes, subtract the **standard deduction** from your income:
- **Single**: $14,600
- **Married Filing Jointly**: $29,200
- **Head of Household**: $21,900
- **65+**: Additional $1,550 (single) or $1,250 (married, per qualifying spouse)

If your total income is $80,000 and you take the standard deduction ($14,600 single), your **taxable income** is $65,400.

## Taxable vs. Gross Income

**Gross Income**: Everything you earned (wages, business income, dividends, etc.)
Minus **"Above the Line" Deductions**: 401(k) contributions, HSA contributions, student loan interest, etc.
= **Adjusted Gross Income (AGI)**
Minus **Standard Deduction or Itemized Deductions**
= **Taxable Income**

Taxable income is what the tax brackets actually apply to — not your total salary.

## Above-the-Line Deductions (Reduce Your AGI)

These are deducted regardless of whether you itemize:
- Traditional 401(k) contributions
- HSA contributions
- Student loan interest (up to $2,500)
- Self-employment taxes (50%)
- IRA contributions (if eligible)

## Itemized Deductions vs. Standard Deduction

You claim whichever is larger:
- **Mortgage interest**
- **State and local taxes (SALT)**: Capped at $10,000
- **Charitable contributions**
- **Medical expenses** exceeding 7.5% of AGI

Most people take the standard deduction since the TCJA 2017 doubled it.

## Key Takeaways

1. The U.S. tax system is progressive — higher rates apply only to income above each threshold
2. Your effective rate is always lower than your marginal rate
3. The standard deduction reduces taxable income before brackets apply
4. Traditional 401(k) and HSA contributions reduce your taxable income directly
