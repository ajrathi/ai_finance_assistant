## What are Tax-Advantaged Accounts?

Tax-advantaged accounts are investment or savings vehicles that receive special tax treatment from the IRS — either reducing taxes now, deferring taxes to the future, or eliminating taxes on growth and withdrawals entirely.

Maximizing these accounts is one of the most powerful strategies for long-term wealth building.

## The Spectrum of Tax Treatment

| Account Type | Contribution Tax | Growth | Withdrawal Tax |
|-------------|-----------------|--------|----------------|
| Traditional 401(k) / IRA | Pre-tax (deductible) | Tax-deferred | Ordinary income |
| Roth 401(k) / IRA | After-tax | Tax-free | Tax-free |
| HSA | Pre-tax | Tax-free | Tax-free (medical) |
| 529 | After-tax | Tax-free | Tax-free (education) |
| Taxable Brokerage | After-tax | Taxable each year | Capital gains rates |

## The Optimal Contribution Order

Financial planning generally recommends this priority:
1. **401(k) up to the employer match** — free money
2. **HSA maximum** — triple tax advantage
3. **Roth IRA maximum** — tax-free growth
4. **401(k) maximum** — remaining limit
5. **Taxable brokerage** — no limits, less favorable taxes

## Account-by-Account Breakdown

### 401(k) / 403(b) — Employer Retirement Plans
- **2024 limit**: $23,000 ($30,500 if 50+)
- **Types**: Traditional (pre-tax) or Roth (after-tax)
- **Employer match**: Often 3–6% of salary — always capture it
- **Investment options**: Limited to plan menu — choose low-cost index funds

### IRA (Individual Retirement Account)
- **2024 limit**: $7,000 ($8,000 if 50+) — shared across all IRAs
- **Types**: Traditional (deductible) or Roth
- **Roth income limits**: Phase out above $146K (single) / $230K (married)
- **Flexibility**: Choose any brokerage, any investment

### HSA (Health Savings Account)
- **2024 limit**: $4,150 individual / $8,300 family
- **Requires**: High-Deductible Health Plan (HDHP) enrollment
- **Triple tax advantage**: Deductible + tax-free growth + tax-free medical withdrawals
- **After 65**: Withdraw for any purpose (taxed as income, like Traditional IRA)

### 529 Education Savings Plan
- **No federal contribution limit** (but gift tax considerations above $18K/year per beneficiary)
- **State tax deductions**: Many states offer deductions for contributions
- **Qualified expenses**: Tuition, books, room and board, K-12 up to $10K/year
- **Flexibility**: Can change beneficiary to another family member
- **Rollover to Roth**: Up to $35,000 can be rolled to beneficiary's Roth IRA after 15 years

### Dependent Care FSA
- **2024 limit**: $5,000/year (per household)
- **For**: Childcare, daycare, summer camp for children under 13
- **Pre-tax**: Reduces payroll taxes too (not just income taxes)
- **Use-it-or-lose-it**: Must use funds within the plan year (grace periods may apply)

### ABLE Account
- For individuals with disabilities (onset before age 26)
- 2024 limit: $18,000/year
- Tax-free growth and withdrawals for disability-related expenses

## Tax Location Strategy

With multiple accounts, consider **where** to hold each investment:

**Tax-deferred accounts (Traditional 401k/IRA)**: High-return, high-tax investments (REITs, bonds generating interest, actively managed funds)

**Roth accounts**: Highest expected growth assets (small-cap stocks, growth stocks) — maximize tax-free compounding

**Taxable accounts**: Tax-efficient investments (index funds, buy-and-hold stocks, municipal bonds)

## 2024 Combined Contribution Potential

A high-income earner with both 401(k) and HSA can shelter:
- 401(k): $23,000
- HSA: $4,150
- IRA: $7,000
- **Total: $34,150** in tax-advantaged contributions (under 50)

## Key Takeaways

1. Prioritize contributions in order: employer match → HSA → Roth IRA → max 401(k) → taxable
2. Traditional accounts defer taxes; Roth accounts create tax-free wealth
3. HSA is the only triple-tax-advantage account
4. 529s grow tax-free for qualified education expenses
5. Tax location (which account holds which investments) further optimizes returns
