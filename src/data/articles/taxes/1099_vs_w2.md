## The Core Difference

The W-2 and 1099 forms represent two fundamentally different working relationships — and dramatically different tax situations.

**W-2 Employee**: Your employer withholds taxes from each paycheck and handles part of your payroll taxes.

**1099 Independent Contractor**: You receive full payment and are responsible for all taxes yourself.

## W-2 Income: How Employee Taxes Work

When you receive a W-2:
- Employer withholds **federal income tax** from each paycheck
- Employer withholds **Social Security tax** (6.2%) from your paycheck
- Employer withholds **Medicare tax** (1.45%) from your paycheck
- Your employer **matches** the Social Security and Medicare taxes (they pay another 6.2% + 1.45%)
- Employer handles **state and local tax** withholding

You typically owe little additional at tax time because withholding has been accurate throughout the year.

## 1099 Income: How Self-Employment Taxes Work

When you receive a 1099-NEC (non-employee compensation) or are self-employed:

**No withholding occurs**: You receive 100% of payment upfront, but you owe:
1. **Self-employment tax (SE tax)**: 15.3% (covers both the employee AND employer portions of Social Security and Medicare)
   - Social Security: 12.4% on net earnings up to $168,600 (2024)
   - Medicare: 2.9% on all net earnings
   - Additional Medicare: 0.9% above $200,000 (single)

2. **Federal income tax**: On all net profit (after business expenses)

3. **State income tax**: Varies by state

**Example:**
- 1099 freelance income: $80,000
- Business expenses: $10,000
- Net profit: $70,000
- SE tax: $70,000 × 92.35% × 15.3% = ~$9,890 (self-employment deduction reduces the taxable base)
- Plus federal income taxes at your bracket rate

## The Self-Employment Deduction

A 1099 worker can deduct **half of the self-employment tax** from gross income (the portion your employer would have paid if you were a W-2 employee). This reduces your taxable income.

## Quarterly Estimated Tax Payments

1099 workers must pay taxes **quarterly** (not annually) to avoid penalties:
- April 15 (for Jan–Mar)
- June 15 (for Apr–May)
- September 15 (for Jun–Aug)
- January 15 of next year (for Sep–Dec)

Failing to make quarterly payments can result in underpayment penalties.

**Safe harbor**: Pay at least 100% of last year's tax liability (or 110% if prior year AGI exceeded $150,000) through estimated payments to avoid penalties.

## Business Deductions for 1099 Workers

Self-employed workers can deduct legitimate business expenses:
- Home office (if used exclusively for business)
- Business use of vehicle (mileage or actual expenses)
- Equipment and software
- Professional development and courses
- Business insurance
- Health insurance premiums (self-employed can deduct 100% of premiums)
- Half of SE tax

Keeping detailed records of expenses is essential.

## Key 1099 Forms

- **1099-NEC**: Non-employee compensation (freelance/contractor income)
- **1099-MISC**: Miscellaneous income (rent, royalties, prizes)
- **1099-INT**: Interest income from banks
- **1099-DIV**: Dividend income from investments
- **1099-B**: Brokerage sales (stocks, bonds)
- **1099-R**: Distributions from retirement accounts
- **1099-K**: Payment card/third-party network transactions (PayPal, Venmo, etc.)

## W-2 vs 1099: At a Glance

| Factor | W-2 Employee | 1099 Contractor |
|--------|-------------|-----------------|
| Tax withholding | Employer withholds | None — you pay quarterly |
| SE tax | 7.65% (employee share) | 15.3% (you pay both sides) |
| Benefits | Employer may provide | Must pay yourself |
| Deductions | Limited | More business deductions available |
| Flexibility | Less | More |
| Job security | More | Less |

## Key Takeaways

1. 1099 workers pay self-employment tax (15.3%) plus income taxes — more than W-2 employees pay directly
2. Quarterly estimated payments are required to avoid penalties
3. 1099 workers can deduct business expenses, including health insurance premiums
4. W-2 employees have taxes withheld automatically — 1099 workers must manage their own
5. Half of the SE tax is deductible from income
