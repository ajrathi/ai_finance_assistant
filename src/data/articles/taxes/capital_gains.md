## What are Capital Gains?

A capital gain is the profit you make when you sell an asset (stock, bond, real estate, etc.) for more than you paid for it.

**Capital Gain = Sale Price − Cost Basis**

Your **cost basis** is typically what you paid for the asset, plus any commissions or fees.

## Short-Term vs. Long-Term Capital Gains

The length of time you hold an asset before selling determines which tax rate applies:

| Holding Period | Type | Tax Rate |
|---------------|------|----------|
| 1 year or less | Short-term | Ordinary income tax rates (up to 37%) |
| More than 1 year | Long-term | Preferential rates (0%, 15%, or 20%) |

## 2024 Long-Term Capital Gains Rates

| Rate | Single Filers | Married Filing Jointly |
|------|--------------|----------------------|
| 0% | $0 – $47,025 | $0 – $94,050 |
| 15% | $47,026 – $518,900 | $94,051 – $583,750 |
| 20% | Over $518,900 | Over $583,750 |

The 0% rate is a significant benefit for low-to-moderate income investors. If your taxable income is in the 0% long-term capital gains bracket, you can sell appreciated investments tax-free.

## The Power of Holding 1 Year+

**Example:**
- You buy 100 shares at $50 ($5,000 total)
- You sell at $100 ($10,000) — a $5,000 gain

If you're in the 22% ordinary income bracket:
- **Hold < 1 year**: $5,000 × 22% = $1,100 in taxes
- **Hold > 1 year**: $5,000 × 15% = $750 in taxes

**Savings: $350** — just by waiting past the one-year mark.

## Capital Losses and Tax-Loss Harvesting

**Capital losses** occur when you sell an asset below its cost basis. These can be used to reduce taxes:

1. **Offset capital gains**: Losses first offset gains dollar-for-dollar
2. **Offset ordinary income**: After offsetting gains, you can deduct up to **$3,000** in remaining losses against ordinary income per year
3. **Carry forward**: Any remaining losses carry forward to future tax years indefinitely

**Tax-loss harvesting**: Strategically selling losing positions to generate deductible losses, while maintaining your investment strategy (by buying a similar — not identical — investment to maintain exposure).

### Wash-Sale Rule
You cannot claim a loss if you buy the same or "substantially identical" security within 30 days before or after the sale. The loss is disallowed (but added to the new position's basis).

## Net Investment Income Tax (NIIT)

High-income taxpayers (above $200,000 single / $250,000 married) pay an additional **3.8% NIIT** on the lesser of:
- Net investment income (capital gains, dividends, interest), OR
- The amount by which their MAGI exceeds the threshold

## Real Estate: Section 121 Exclusion

When selling your primary residence, you can exclude up to:
- **$250,000** in capital gains (single)
- **$500,000** in capital gains (married filing jointly)

Requirements: Owned and used as primary residence for 2 of the last 5 years.

## Strategies to Minimize Capital Gains Tax

1. **Hold investments over 1 year** to qualify for long-term rates
2. **Tax-loss harvesting** to offset gains
3. **Invest in tax-advantaged accounts** (401k, IRA) — no capital gains taxes within the account
4. **Donate appreciated assets to charity** — avoid gains entirely and get a deduction
5. **Step-up in basis** — inherited assets receive a cost basis equal to market value at death (eliminating embedded gains)

## Key Takeaways

1. Hold assets >1 year for lower long-term capital gains rates (0%, 15%, 20%)
2. Short-term gains are taxed as ordinary income (up to 37%)
3. Capital losses offset gains and up to $3,000 of ordinary income per year
4. The wash-sale rule prevents claiming losses on "substantially identical" repurchases
5. Tax-advantaged accounts (401k, IRA) shelter investments from capital gains taxes
