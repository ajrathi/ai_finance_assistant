## What is Tax-Loss Harvesting?

Tax-loss harvesting (TLH) is a strategy where you **intentionally sell investments at a loss** to generate a tax deduction, then immediately reinvest in a similar (but not identical) security to maintain your market exposure.

The goal: offset capital gains taxes while staying invested in the market.

## How It Works

1. You own Investment A, currently worth $7,000 (you bought it for $10,000) — a $3,000 unrealized loss
2. You sell Investment A and recognize the $3,000 loss
3. Immediately buy Investment B (similar asset, different fund) to stay invested
4. The $3,000 loss offsets $3,000 in capital gains elsewhere
5. If no gains to offset, deduct up to $3,000 against ordinary income ($1,500 if married filing separately)
6. Remaining losses carry forward to future years

## The Tax Benefit

**Without TLH:**
- $10,000 in capital gains × 15% LTCG rate = $1,500 in taxes

**With TLH (using $3,000 in harvested losses):**
- $10,000 gains − $3,000 losses = $7,000 net gains
- $7,000 × 15% = $1,050 in taxes
- **Tax savings: $450**

The harvested losses effectively defer taxation until later (when you sell the replacement investment).

## The Wash-Sale Rule: The Critical Constraint

The IRS's wash-sale rule prevents abuse: **You cannot claim a loss if you buy a "substantially identical" security within 30 days before or after the sale.**

**Examples:**
- Selling Vanguard S&P 500 ETF (VOO) and buying SPDR S&P 500 ETF (SPY) the next day: **WASH SALE** (both track the same index)
- Selling VOO and buying Vanguard Total Market ETF (VTI): **Not a wash sale** (different index, different holdings)
- Selling individual Apple stock and buying an Apple-heavy ETF: Generally **not** a wash sale (different securities)

If a wash sale occurs, the disallowed loss is added to the cost basis of the new purchase — it's not gone, just delayed.

## When TLH Makes Sense

✅ **Good candidates for TLH:**
- You have capital gains to offset in the same or future years
- You're in the 15% or 20% LTCG bracket
- Markets have had a significant downturn creating losses in your portfolio
- You have a taxable brokerage account (not applicable in tax-advantaged accounts)

❌ **TLH is less valuable when:**
- You're in the 0% LTCG bracket (no gains tax anyway)
- You're near death (step-up in basis at death eliminates embedded gains)
- The transaction costs and complexity outweigh the tax savings

## TLH is Tax Deferral, Not Permanent Tax Elimination

When you eventually sell Investment B (the replacement), you'll pay taxes on gains that include the harvested loss period. The benefit is:
1. Tax deferral (money stays invested longer)
2. Potential rate arbitrage (if you're in a lower bracket later)
3. Potential permanent elimination (if you die holding the asset — step-up in basis)

## Automated Tax-Loss Harvesting

Several robo-advisors (Betterment, Wealthfront) offer automated TLH as a feature — they monitor portfolios daily and harvest losses when they appear. This high-frequency harvesting can add 0.10–0.77% in annual after-tax returns according to various studies.

For large portfolios, the tax savings from systematic TLH can significantly outweigh robo-advisor fees.

## Practical Example: A Market Correction

In October 2022, many tech stocks fell 30–50%:
- You hold $20,000 in Technology ETF A (bought at $30,000) — unrealized loss of $10,000
- You sell and immediately buy Technology ETF B (similar but different index)
- You recognize $10,000 in losses
- These offset $10,000 in other capital gains, saving you up to $2,000 in taxes (at 20% rate)
- You remain invested and participate in the eventual recovery

## Key Takeaways

1. TLH = sell at a loss, buy similar asset, use loss to offset capital gains
2. The wash-sale rule prohibits repurchasing "substantially identical" securities within 30 days
3. Best for investors in the 15–20% capital gains bracket with gains to offset
4. Only applicable in taxable brokerage accounts (not 401k/IRA — no capital gains taxes there)
5. It's tax deferral, not permanent elimination — gains on the replacement investment are taxable
