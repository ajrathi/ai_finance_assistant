## The Two Main Debt Payoff Methods

When you have multiple debts, two popular strategies can help you become debt-free faster:

1. **Debt Avalanche**: Pay off the highest-interest debt first
2. **Debt Snowball**: Pay off the smallest balance first

Both require the same foundation: pay the minimum on all debts, then put any extra money toward your target debt.

## Debt Avalanche Method

**Strategy**: List debts by interest rate, highest to lowest. Direct extra payments to the highest-rate debt while paying minimums on the rest. When the highest-rate debt is gone, roll its payment to the next highest.

**Example:**
| Debt | Balance | Interest Rate | Min. Payment |
|------|---------|--------------|--------------|
| Credit Card A | $5,000 | 22% | $100 |
| Personal Loan | $8,000 | 12% | $200 |
| Car Loan | $12,000 | 5% | $250 |

**Avalanche order**: Credit Card A → Personal Loan → Car Loan

**Advantage**: Mathematically optimal — you pay the least total interest over time.

**Disadvantage**: If the highest-rate debt is large, you may go months without eliminating a debt, which can feel discouraging.

## Debt Snowball Method

**Strategy**: List debts by balance, smallest to largest. Direct extra payments to the smallest balance regardless of interest rate. When it's paid off, roll that payment to the next smallest.

Using the same example:
**Snowball order**: Credit Card A → Personal Loan → Car Loan
(Coincidentally the same here, but imagine Credit Card A had a $1,000 balance — it would be paid off first under snowball regardless of its rate.)

**Advantage**: Quick wins build momentum and motivation. Psychological research (notably by Dave Ramsey) shows most people stick with snowball better.

**Disadvantage**: You may pay more interest overall compared to avalanche.

## Which Method Wins?

**Mathematically**: Avalanche (saves more money)
**Behaviorally**: Snowball (many people stay with it longer)

The best method is the one **you'll actually stick to**. A perfect strategy abandoned after two months is worse than a slightly suboptimal strategy you follow consistently.

## The Debt Roll-Up Effect

Both methods rely on the "roll-up" or "debt snowball/avalanche roll":

- As each debt is eliminated, redirect its minimum payment to the next debt
- Your attack on remaining debts grows larger over time
- Total monthly debt payments stay constant, but the power behind each target debt grows

## Other Debt Considerations

### High-Interest First (Always)

For any debt above 7-8% interest rate, paying it off is typically better than investing. A 22% credit card guaranteed "return" on payoff beats uncertain 7% market returns.

### Minimum Emergency Fund First

Before aggressive debt payoff, maintain at least $1,000 emergency fund to avoid going further into debt for unexpected expenses.

### Refinancing Options

- **Balance transfer cards**: Some credit cards offer 0% APR for 12-18 months on transferred balances (fees apply)
- **Personal loan consolidation**: Roll high-interest credit card debt into a lower-rate personal loan
- **Student loan refinancing**: Can reduce interest rate, but may lose income-driven repayment or forgiveness options

## Red Flags of Debt

Signs debt has become a crisis:
- Using credit cards for basic necessities
- Only paying minimums every month
- Taking payday loans
- Hiding debt from a partner
- More than 20% of take-home pay going to non-mortgage debt payments

## Key Takeaways

1. Avalanche saves the most money; Snowball provides psychological momentum
2. Both require paying minimums on all debts and extra on the target debt
3. The best method is the one you'll stick to consistently
4. Eliminate high-interest debt (>7%) before investing beyond the employer match
5. Roll each paid-off debt's payment to the next target — the momentum builds

*For educational purposes only. Debt situations vary widely. Consider consulting a nonprofit credit counselor for complex situations.*
