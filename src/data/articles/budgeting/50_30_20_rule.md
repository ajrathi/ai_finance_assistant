## What is the 50/30/20 Rule?

The 50/30/20 rule is a simple, flexible budgeting framework popularized by Senator Elizabeth Warren in her book "All Your Worth." It divides your **after-tax income** into three broad categories:

- **50%** — Needs (essential expenses)
- **30%** — Wants (discretionary spending)
- **20%** — Savings and debt repayment

## After-Tax Income: The Starting Point

Use your **take-home pay** (after federal taxes, state taxes, and Social Security are withheld). If you earn $5,000/month after taxes:
- 50% needs = $2,500
- 30% wants = $1,500
- 20% savings = $1,000

## The 50%: Needs

Needs are expenses you cannot avoid — the basics required to live and work.

**Examples:**
- Rent or mortgage payment
- Utilities (electricity, water, internet)
- Groceries (basic food)
- Transportation (car payment, gas, or transit fare)
- Minimum debt payments
- Basic insurance (health, car, renters/homeowners)
- Childcare

**Not needs:** A premium cable package, dining out, gym membership, or a new car upgrade.

If your needs exceed 50% of income, look for ways to reduce costs: roommates, cheaper housing, refinancing, or increasing income.

## The 30%: Wants

Wants are everything beyond the basics — things that improve your quality of life but aren't strictly necessary.

**Examples:**
- Dining out and takeout
- Entertainment (streaming, concerts, movies)
- Gym membership
- Shopping (clothing beyond basics)
- Vacations and travel
- Hobbies
- Subscriptions (music, apps)
- Upgraded products/services

This category is where most lifestyle inflation happens. Be honest about distinguishing wants from needs.

## The 20%: Savings and Debt Repayment

This is your wealth-building and financial security category.

**Priority order:**
1. Emergency fund (until 3–6 months of expenses saved)
2. High-interest debt repayment (credit cards, payday loans)
3. Employer 401(k) match (free money — prioritize this)
4. Retirement savings (IRA, additional 401(k))
5. Other goals (down payment, education, etc.)

If you have high-interest debt, consider allocating more than 20% to debt repayment temporarily.

## Adapting the Rule to Your Situation

The 50/30/20 rule is a guideline, not a rigid law. Adjust as needed:

**High cost-of-living area:** Needs may take 60–65% of income — reduce wants accordingly.

**Aggressive debt payoff:** Temporarily increase savings/debt to 30–40%, cut wants.

**Near retirement:** Increase savings to 30%+ — the more you save, the earlier you can retire.

**Low income:** May need to prioritize needs, with whatever remains going to savings.

## Tracking Your Spending

To use the 50/30/20 rule:
1. Calculate your monthly after-tax income
2. Categorize last month's expenses
3. See where you stand vs. the target
4. Adjust as needed

**Tools:** Budgeting apps (Mint, YNAB, Personal Capital), bank categorization features, or a simple spreadsheet.

## Alternative Budget Methods

**Zero-based budgeting**: Allocate every dollar to a purpose until income minus expenses = $0. More granular control.

**Envelope method**: Physically (or digitally) separate money for each spending category.

**Pay yourself first**: Automate savings before spending anything. Budget around what remains.

## Key Takeaways

1. Split after-tax income: 50% needs, 30% wants, 20% savings/debt
2. Be honest about needs vs. wants — most luxuries are wants
3. The 20% savings category builds your financial future
4. Adjust percentages based on your situation and goals
5. Tracking spending is the key to making any budget work
