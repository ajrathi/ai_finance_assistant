## What is Zero-Based Budgeting?

Zero-based budgeting (ZBB) is a method where you allocate every dollar of your income to a specific category until:

**Income − All Allocations = $0**

This doesn't mean you spend everything — savings and debt payoff are categories too. The "zero" means every dollar has a purpose.

## Core Principle

Traditional budgeting asks: "How much did I spend?"
Zero-based budgeting asks: "Where will each dollar go before I spend it?"

It transforms budgeting from a tracking exercise into a planning exercise.

## How to Set Up a Zero-Based Budget

### Step 1: Calculate Monthly Income
Use your actual take-home pay (after taxes). For variable income, use a conservative estimate (last month's actual or average of last 3 months).

### Step 2: List All Monthly Expenses

**Fixed expenses** (same each month):
- Rent/mortgage
- Car payment
- Insurance premiums
- Subscriptions

**Variable necessities**:
- Groceries (estimate)
- Utilities
- Gas
- Medical co-pays

**Irregular expenses** (divide annual cost by 12):
- Car registration: $300/year = $25/month (put it aside)
- Annual subscriptions: $120/year = $10/month
- Holiday gifts: $600/year = $50/month

**Financial goals**:
- Emergency fund contribution
- Retirement contribution
- Debt payoff
- Vacation fund

**Discretionary**:
- Dining out
- Entertainment
- Clothing
- Personal care

### Step 3: Subtract All Categories from Income
Keep adjusting amounts until income − expenses = $0.

If you're $200 over, reduce a discretionary category. If $200 under, allocate to savings or debt.

## The Power of Irregular Expense Planning

Most budgets fail because of irregular expenses (car repair, medical bills, holidays). ZBB handles these by creating "sinking funds":

- Each month, set aside 1/12 of your annual irregular costs
- When the expense hits, the money is already there
- Eliminates "budget-busting" surprises

**Example sinking funds:**
- Car maintenance: $600/year = $50/month
- Medical copays: $500/year = $42/month
- Holidays/gifts: $1,200/year = $100/month
- Vacation: $2,400/year = $200/month

## Zero-Based vs. 50/30/20

| Aspect | Zero-Based | 50/30/20 |
|--------|------------|----------|
| Granularity | Very detailed | Broad categories |
| Time to set up | 30–60 minutes initially | 5–10 minutes |
| Flexibility | Lower (more rigid) | Higher |
| Best for | Debt payoff, tight budgets | Those wanting simple framework |
| Behavior change | High (more intentional) | Moderate |

## Common Pitfalls

**Forgetting irregular expenses**: Use a full-year list. In January, write down every expense you anticipate in the coming year.

**Being too restrictive**: If your dining out budget is $0, you'll abandon the budget. Budget realistically.

**Not adjusting mid-month**: Life happens. When you overspend in one category, subtract from another (on-budget adjustment). This is normal and part of the method.

**Multiple income earners**: Both partners should agree on and build the budget together.

## Digital Tools

**YNAB (You Need A Budget)**: The most popular zero-based budgeting app. Subscription-based, but users report it pays for itself many times over.

**Spreadsheets**: A simple monthly spreadsheet works well. Many free templates available.

**Pen and paper**: Still effective for those who prefer physical budgeting.

## The Psychological Benefit

When you intentionally decide where money goes in advance, you make different decisions than when you track spending after the fact. You become more intentional about purchases because you feel the trade-off: "If I spend $50 on this, it comes from my vacation fund."

## Key Takeaways

1. Every dollar is assigned a purpose until income minus all allocations = $0
2. Savings and debt payoff are categories — not "whatever is left over"
3. Sinking funds handle irregular expenses without surprises
4. More time-intensive than 50/30/20 but more effective for behavior change
5. YNAB is the most popular digital tool for zero-based budgeting
