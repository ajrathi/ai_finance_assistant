## What is an Index Fund?

An index fund is a type of investment fund that tracks a specific market index — like the S&P 500, Dow Jones, or NASDAQ Composite. Instead of trying to "beat the market," it aims to match it.

When you invest in an S&P 500 index fund, you automatically own a slice of all 500 companies in that index, proportional to their market size.

## How Index Funds Work

1. A company (like Vanguard, Fidelity, or BlackRock) creates a fund
2. The fund buys shares of every company in the target index
3. Investors buy shares of the fund
4. As the index rises, fund value rises (and vice versa)
5. The fund rebalances automatically when the index changes

## Index Funds vs. Actively Managed Funds

| Feature | Index Fund | Actively Managed Fund |
|---------|------------|-----------------------|
| Strategy | Match the market | Beat the market |
| Management | Passive (computer-driven) | Active (human managers) |
| Expense Ratio | 0.03%–0.20% | 0.50%–2.00%+ |
| Tax Efficiency | High | Lower (more trading) |
| Typical Performance | ~Market returns | Often underperforms market |

**The math on fees**: A 1% fee difference on $100,000 over 30 years at 7% growth costs you approximately $120,000 in lost wealth.

## The Case for Index Investing

Academic research consistently shows that most actively managed funds underperform their benchmark index after fees over 10+ year periods.

Key study: **SPIVA (S&P Indices Versus Active)** reports annually show 80–90% of actively managed funds underperform their index over 15 years.

This is why John Bogle (founder of Vanguard) and Warren Buffett both recommend index funds for most investors.

## Common Types of Index Funds

### By Structure
- **Mutual funds**: Bought/sold at end-of-day price (NAV)
- **ETFs (Exchange-Traded Funds)**: Traded throughout the day on exchanges like stocks

### By Coverage
- **Total Market**: All U.S. stocks (e.g., CRSP Total Market Index)
- **S&P 500**: 500 largest U.S. companies
- **International**: Developed or emerging market stocks
- **Bond Index**: U.S. or international bonds
- **Sector**: Technology, healthcare, real estate, etc.

## Expense Ratios

The expense ratio is the annual fee charged by the fund, expressed as a percentage of assets.

- Vanguard S&P 500 Index Fund (VFIAX): ~0.04%
- Fidelity ZERO Total Market: 0.00%
- Typical actively managed fund: 0.50%–1.50%

On $10,000:
- 0.04% fee = $4/year
- 1.00% fee = $100/year
- Over 30 years, that difference compounds significantly

## Dollar-Cost Averaging with Index Funds

Investing a fixed amount regularly (monthly), regardless of market conditions, is called **dollar-cost averaging (DCA)**. It removes the temptation to time the market and ensures you buy more shares when prices are low and fewer when high.

## Key Takeaways

1. Index funds track a market benchmark, not try to beat it
2. Low expense ratios are a primary advantage
3. Most active managers underperform their index long-term
4. Available as mutual funds or ETFs
5. Ideal for passive, long-term investors
