## What is Diversification?

Diversification means spreading your investments across different assets, sectors, and geographies so that no single investment can significantly harm your overall portfolio.

The classic analogy: Don't put all your eggs in one basket. If you drop one basket, you lose everything. With multiple baskets, one drop is manageable.

## Why Diversification Works

Different assets often don't move together (they have low **correlation**). When stocks fall, bonds often rise. When U.S. stocks struggle, international stocks may perform better. This offsetting behavior reduces portfolio volatility.

**Correlation coefficient** ranges from -1 to +1:
- **+1**: Assets move together perfectly (no diversification benefit)
- **0**: No relationship
- **-1**: Assets move perfectly opposite (maximum diversification)

## Types of Diversification

### Asset Class Diversification
Spread across different types of assets:
- Stocks (equities)
- Bonds (fixed income)
- Real estate (REITs)
- Cash/money market
- Commodities

### Geographic Diversification
Spread across different countries and regions:
- U.S. stocks
- International developed markets (Europe, Japan)
- Emerging markets (China, India, Brazil)

### Sector Diversification
Within stocks, spread across industries:
- Technology
- Healthcare
- Financial services
- Consumer staples
- Energy
- Utilities

### Company Diversification
Don't concentrate in a single company. Owning 20–30 stocks significantly reduces individual company risk. Index funds provide instant diversification across hundreds or thousands of companies.

## The Limits of Diversification

### Systematic Risk (Cannot Be Diversified Away)
Market-wide risks affect all investments:
- Recessions
- Interest rate changes
- Inflation
- Geopolitical events

### Unsystematic Risk (Can Be Diversified Away)
Company or sector-specific risks:
- Company bankruptcy
- Industry disruption
- Management scandals

Diversification only eliminates **unsystematic risk**. You can't diversify away market crashes.

## Over-Diversification

Too many holdings create their own problems:
- Diluted returns (your best performers' impact is minimized)
- Higher costs if using many individual funds
- Complexity without benefit

Research suggests most unsystematic risk is eliminated with 20–30 well-chosen stocks. Beyond that, returns start to mirror the index.

## Simple Diversification with Index Funds

A three-fund portfolio is a popular simple approach:
1. **U.S. Total Stock Market Index Fund** (domestic equities)
2. **International Stock Market Index Fund** (foreign equities)
3. **U.S. Bond Market Index Fund** (fixed income)

This covers thousands of securities across markets with just three funds.

## The 60/40 Portfolio

A traditional diversified portfolio:
- 60% stocks (growth)
- 40% bonds (stability)

Historically, this balanced risk and return for moderate investors. Younger investors often shift toward 80–90% stocks; those near retirement shift toward more bonds.

## Key Takeaways

1. Diversification reduces risk without proportionally reducing expected returns
2. Spread across asset classes, geographies, and sectors
3. Index funds provide instant, low-cost diversification
4. You cannot diversify away market-wide (systematic) risk
5. Over-diversification is possible — focus on meaningful diversification

*For educational purposes only. Diversification does not guarantee a profit or protect against loss.*
