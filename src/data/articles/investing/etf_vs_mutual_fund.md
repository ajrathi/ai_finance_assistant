## Two Ways to Own a Basket of Securities

Both **ETFs** (Exchange-Traded Funds) and **mutual funds** pool money from many investors to purchase a diversified basket of securities. The key differences are in how they're structured and traded.

## ETF (Exchange-Traded Fund)

An ETF is bought and sold on a stock exchange throughout the trading day, just like individual stocks. You need a brokerage account to buy ETFs.

**Key features:**
- **Intraday trading**: Prices fluctuate throughout the trading day
- **No minimums**: You can buy a single share (or even fractional shares)
- **Generally lower expense ratios**: Competition has driven costs extremely low
- **Tax efficient**: "In-kind" redemptions typically avoid triggering capital gains
- **Transparent**: Holdings usually disclosed daily

## Mutual Fund

A mutual fund is priced once per day after market close (at NAV — Net Asset Value). All buy/sell orders execute at that day's closing price.

**Key features:**
- **End-of-day pricing**: All transactions at 4pm NAV
- **Minimums**: Many require $1,000–$3,000 to open
- **Can be actively managed**: Many mutual funds have professional managers selecting securities
- **Automatic investment**: Easy to set up recurring contributions of exact dollar amounts
- **Less tax efficient**: Managers' trading can generate taxable capital gains distributions

## Side-by-Side Comparison

| Feature | ETF | Mutual Fund |
|---------|-----|-------------|
| Trading | Throughout trading day | Once daily (4pm NAV) |
| Minimum investment | Price of 1 share (or fractional) | Usually $1,000–$3,000 |
| Expense ratios | Often 0.03–0.20% | 0.10% (index) to 2%+ (active) |
| Tax efficiency | Higher | Lower |
| Automatic investment | Harder (fixed $/month requires fractional shares) | Easy (any dollar amount) |
| Types | Mostly passive (index-tracking) | Both passive and active |
| Management | Usually passive | Often active |

## Index ETFs vs. Index Mutual Funds

When both track the same index, the differences shrink considerably:

**Vanguard Total Stock Market:**
- As ETF (VTI): 0.03% expense ratio, intraday trading
- As Mutual Fund (VTSAX): 0.04% expense ratio, automatic investment easy, $3,000 minimum

For most long-term investors, the difference is minimal. Choose based on convenience and your preference for automatic contributions vs. intraday flexibility.

## When to Choose an ETF

- You want intraday trading flexibility
- You want the lowest possible expense ratio
- You're investing a lump sum
- You want fractional shares (at brokerages offering them)
- You want maximum tax efficiency

## When to Choose a Mutual Fund

- You want automatic monthly investments of exact dollar amounts (like $500/month)
- You're investing in a 401(k) (often mutual funds only)
- You want active management (though most evidence suggests it's not worth the cost)
- You prefer the simplicity of "set it and forget it" automatic rebalancing features

## The Role of the Fund Company

Both ETFs and mutual funds are managed by fund companies:
- **Vanguard**: Known for lowest costs, investor-owned structure
- **Fidelity**: Offers zero-expense-ratio index funds
- **BlackRock (iShares)**: Largest ETF provider globally
- **Schwab**: Competitive low-cost options

## Key Takeaways

1. ETFs trade on exchanges like stocks; mutual funds price once daily
2. Both can track the same index with nearly identical performance
3. ETFs are slightly more tax-efficient; mutual funds are easier for automatic investing
4. For most investors, choosing between index ETFs and index mutual funds matters less than choosing low-cost index products
5. Avoid high-fee actively managed funds — they consistently underperform indexes long-term
