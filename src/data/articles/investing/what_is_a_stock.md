## What is a Stock?

A stock (also called a **share** or **equity**) represents partial ownership in a company. When you buy a stock, you become a **shareholder** — a part-owner of that business.

If Apple has 15 billion shares outstanding and you own 100 shares, you own 0.0000007% of Apple. That fraction entitles you to a proportional claim on the company's assets and earnings.

## How Stocks Make You Money

### 1. Capital Appreciation
The stock price rises over time as the company grows. You buy at $100, sell at $150 — a $50 gain (50% return).

### 2. Dividends
Some companies distribute a portion of profits to shareholders as **dividends** (typically quarterly cash payments). Dividend yield = Annual dividend ÷ Stock price.
- A $50 stock paying $2/year in dividends = 4% dividend yield

**Growth stocks** (like tech companies) typically reinvest profits rather than pay dividends, aiming for stock price appreciation.
**Value/income stocks** (utilities, REITs) tend to pay higher dividends.

## Stock Markets

Stocks are bought and sold on exchanges:
- **NYSE** (New York Stock Exchange): Oldest U.S. exchange, home to large established companies
- **NASDAQ**: Technology-heavy exchange

The **S&P 500** is an index of 500 large U.S. companies, widely used as a benchmark for the stock market.

## Key Concepts

### Market Capitalization
Total value of a company's outstanding shares.
- Market Cap = Share Price × Shares Outstanding
- Apple at $190/share × 15B shares = ~$2.85 trillion market cap

### Categories:
- **Large-cap**: >$10B (Apple, Microsoft)
- **Mid-cap**: $2B–$10B
- **Small-cap**: <$2B (higher growth potential, higher risk)

## Risks of Owning Stocks

**Market risk**: Stock prices fluctuate based on company performance, economic conditions, investor sentiment. Prices can drop 50%+ during recessions.

**Company risk**: A single company can go bankrupt, making its stock worthless. Diversification (owning many stocks) mitigates this.

**Volatility**: Individual stocks can swing 5–20% in a single day on news. This is normal but can be emotionally challenging.

## Common Stock Metrics

- **P/E Ratio**: Price ÷ Earnings per share. Higher = more expensive relative to earnings
- **EPS**: Earnings per share (company profit divided by shares outstanding)
- **Beta**: Measures volatility vs. the overall market (beta >1 = more volatile)

## Key Takeaways

1. A stock = partial ownership in a company
2. Returns come from price appreciation and/or dividends
3. Individual stocks carry company-specific risk — diversify
4. Long-term stock market investing has historically outpaced inflation
