## What is Dollar-Cost Averaging?

Dollar-cost averaging (DCA) is the practice of investing a fixed dollar amount at regular intervals — regardless of market conditions. Instead of trying to time the market, you invest consistently over time.

**Example:** Investing $500 every month into an S&P 500 index fund, every month for years, regardless of whether the market is up or down.

## How DCA Works in Practice

| Month | Investment | Share Price | Shares Bought |
|-------|-----------|-------------|---------------|
| Jan | $500 | $50 | 10.0 |
| Feb | $500 | $40 | 12.5 |
| Mar | $500 | $25 | 20.0 |
| Apr | $500 | $40 | 12.5 |
| May | $500 | $50 | 10.0 |

**Total invested:** $2,500
**Total shares:** 65 shares
**Current price:** $50
**Portfolio value:** $3,250
**Average cost per share:** $38.46 (vs. buying all at $50 = much higher cost)

When the price dropped, you automatically bought **more** shares. This lowers your average cost per share over time.

## DCA vs. Lump Sum Investing

**Lump sum**: Invest all available money at once.
**DCA**: Spread investments over time.

Research shows that lump sum investing outperforms DCA about two-thirds of the time (because markets tend to go up over time, and waiting means you miss early gains). However, DCA wins during volatile or declining markets.

**The real argument for DCA isn't performance — it's behavior.**

Most people don't have a large lump sum. They earn income regularly and invest regularly. DCA is simply how most people invest in practice (e.g., contributing to a 401k each paycheck).

## Psychological Benefits of DCA

1. **Removes emotion**: No need to decide "Is now a good time to invest?"
2. **Eliminates market timing**: You can't time the market consistently
3. **Automates discipline**: Set it and forget it
4. **Reduces regret**: If you invest a lump sum and the market drops immediately, you'll feel bad. DCA spreads this risk.

## DCA with a 401(k)

Your 401(k) contributions already use DCA. Each paycheck, a fixed percentage is automatically invested — regardless of what the market is doing. This is DCA in practice.

## Combining DCA with Automatic Rebalancing

Set up automatic monthly investments AND annual rebalancing (restoring your target allocation). This keeps your portfolio on track without constant monitoring.

## The Myth of Waiting for the "Perfect" Entry Point

Research by Charles Schwab showed that even an investor with terrible timing (always buying at market peaks) significantly outperformed an investor who stayed in cash waiting for the right moment. Time in the market beats timing the market.

## Implementation

1. **Set a fixed amount**: What can you consistently invest monthly?
2. **Choose your investment**: Low-cost index fund (e.g., S&P 500)
3. **Automate**: Set up automatic monthly transfers
4. **Don't watch daily**: Ignore short-term fluctuations
5. **Stay consistent**: Especially during market drops

## Key Takeaways

1. DCA = fixed investment amount at regular intervals
2. You automatically buy more shares when prices are low
3. Removes the need to time the market
4. Your 401(k) contributions are already DCA
5. Consistency over time matters more than perfect timing

*For educational purposes only. Regular investing does not guarantee profit or prevent losses.*
