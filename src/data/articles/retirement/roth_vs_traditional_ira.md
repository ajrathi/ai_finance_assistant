## What is an IRA?

An **Individual Retirement Account (IRA)** is a tax-advantaged account you open independently of your employer. Unlike a 401(k), you control the IRA — you choose the brokerage and investment options available.

## 2024 IRA Contribution Limits

- **Under 50**: $7,000/year
- **50 or older**: $8,000/year (includes $1,000 catch-up)

These limits are per person and are shared across ALL your IRAs combined (you can't contribute $7,000 to a Roth AND $7,000 to a Traditional IRA in the same year).

## Traditional IRA

### Tax Treatment
- Contributions may be **tax-deductible** (reduces taxable income this year)
- Growth is **tax-deferred**
- Withdrawals in retirement are taxed as **ordinary income**

### Deductibility Rules
If you (or your spouse) have a 401(k) at work, your ability to deduct Traditional IRA contributions phases out at certain income levels.

### Required Minimum Distributions (RMDs)
You must begin withdrawals at **age 73**. The IRS requires this so they can collect taxes on the tax-deferred growth.

## Roth IRA

### Tax Treatment
- Contributions are **not tax-deductible** (no immediate tax break)
- Growth is **tax-free**
- Qualified withdrawals in retirement are **completely tax-free**

### Income Limits (2024)
You can only contribute to a Roth IRA if your income is below certain thresholds:
- **Single**: Full contribution below $146,000, phase-out up to $161,000
- **Married filing jointly**: Full contribution below $230,000, phase-out up to $240,000

### No RMDs
Roth IRAs have NO required minimum distributions during your lifetime. Your money can compound tax-free indefinitely.

### Withdrawal Flexibility
Roth IRA **contributions** (not earnings) can be withdrawn anytime without taxes or penalties — making it a flexible emergency backup.

## Roth vs. Traditional: The Decision Framework

**Choose Roth IRA if:**
- You expect to be in a higher tax bracket in retirement than now
- You're early in your career with a lower current income
- You want tax-free withdrawals and no RMDs
- You want flexibility to access contributions if needed

**Choose Traditional IRA if:**
- You're in a high tax bracket now and expect to be lower in retirement
- You need the current year tax deduction
- Your income exceeds Roth limits (see Backdoor Roth below)

## The Backdoor Roth IRA

High-income earners above the Roth limit can still access a Roth IRA through a two-step process:
1. Contribute to a non-deductible Traditional IRA
2. Convert it to a Roth IRA

This is legal and commonly used, but has tax implications — consult a tax professional.

## Investment Options in IRAs

Unlike 401(k)s, IRAs let you invest in virtually anything:
- Individual stocks and bonds
- ETFs and mutual funds
- REITs
- Options (at some brokerages)

Major IRA providers: Vanguard, Fidelity, Schwab, and online brokerages.

## Key Takeaways

1. IRA 2024 limit: $7,000 ($8,000 if 50+)
2. Roth = tax-free in retirement; Traditional = tax deduction now
3. Roth has income limits; Traditional does not (but deductibility does)
4. Roth has no RMDs; Traditional requires withdrawals at 73
5. If income exceeds Roth limits, consider the backdoor Roth strategy
