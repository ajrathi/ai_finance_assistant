## What is an HSA?

A Health Savings Account (HSA) is a tax-advantaged account for medical expenses — but it's also one of the most powerful investment and retirement savings vehicles available. It offers a unique **triple tax advantage** that no other account type can match.

## The Triple Tax Advantage

1. **Contributions are tax-deductible** (reduce taxable income)
2. **Growth is tax-free** (investments grow without tax)
3. **Withdrawals for qualified medical expenses are tax-free**

No other account type — not a 401(k), not a Roth IRA — offers all three benefits simultaneously.

## Eligibility Requirements

To contribute to an HSA, you must be enrolled in a **High-Deductible Health Plan (HDHP)**:
- **2024 HDHP minimum deductible**: $1,600 (individual) / $3,200 (family)
- **2024 HDHP out-of-pocket maximum**: $8,050 (individual) / $16,100 (family)

You cannot be enrolled in Medicare or claimed as a dependent.

## 2024 Contribution Limits

- **Individual coverage**: $4,150/year
- **Family coverage**: $8,300/year
- **Age 55+ catch-up**: Additional $1,000/year

## How to Use an HSA

### Option 1: Pay Medical Expenses Now
Use HSA funds for current qualified medical expenses tax-free:
- Doctor visits, prescriptions, dental, vision
- Mental health services
- Long-term care premiums

### Option 2: Invest and Let it Grow (Stealth Retirement Account)
Pay medical expenses out-of-pocket now, invest HSA contributions, let them grow for decades. After 65, HSA withdrawals for ANY purpose (not just medical) are taxed as ordinary income — just like a Traditional IRA. But for medical expenses, withdrawals remain tax-free forever.

This strategy effectively turns the HSA into a bonus IRA with superior tax treatment.

## HSA as a Retirement Healthcare Fund

Healthcare is one of the largest retirement expenses. Fidelity estimates that an average retired couple needs approximately $315,000 for healthcare costs in retirement (2023 estimate). An HSA invested in index funds over 20–30 years can grow significantly to help cover this.

## Key Rules

- **Funds roll over**: Unlike FSAs, HSA funds don't expire annually
- **Portable**: The account follows you — not your employer or health plan
- **No "use it or lose it"**: Unused funds keep growing
- **Invest after minimum balance**: Most HSA providers allow investing once balance exceeds ~$1,000–$2,000

## After Age 65

- Medical expense withdrawals: Tax-free (same as before)
- Non-medical withdrawals: Taxable as ordinary income (no 20% penalty)
- Effectively works like a Traditional IRA for non-medical expenses

## Common HSA Investing Strategy

1. Contribute maximum to HSA annually
2. Pay all medical expenses out-of-pocket (save receipts!)
3. Invest HSA funds in low-cost index funds
4. In retirement, use HSA for tax-free medical expenses
5. Keep all receipts from current medical expenses — you can reimburse yourself years later with no time limit

## Key Takeaways

1. HSA = triple tax advantage: deductible contributions + tax-free growth + tax-free medical withdrawals
2. Requires enrollment in a High-Deductible Health Plan (HDHP)
3. 2024 limits: $4,150 (individual) / $8,300 (family)
4. Funds never expire — roll over indefinitely
5. After 65, non-medical withdrawals taxed like a Traditional IRA

*For educational purposes only. Tax rules are complex and subject to change. Consult a tax professional.*
