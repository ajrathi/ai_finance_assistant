## What is a 401(k)?

A 401(k) is a tax-advantaged retirement savings account offered by employers. The name comes from the section of the IRS tax code that established it. You contribute pre-tax dollars from your paycheck, reducing your taxable income today, and your investments grow tax-deferred until withdrawal in retirement.

## How it Works

1. You elect to contribute a percentage of your paycheck
2. Contributions are deducted pre-tax (Traditional 401k) or post-tax (Roth 401k)
3. Your employer may match a portion of your contributions (free money!)
4. Funds are invested in options chosen from your plan's menu
5. At retirement (59½+), you withdraw funds and pay income tax (Traditional) or tax-free (Roth)

## 2024 Contribution Limits

- **Under 50**: $23,000/year
- **50 or older**: $30,500/year (includes $7,500 catch-up contribution)

These limits apply to your contributions only. Employer contributions are additional.

## The Employer Match: Free Money

Many employers match contributions up to a percentage of your salary. This is essentially free money — the highest guaranteed "return" available.

**Example:** Employer matches 100% of contributions up to 3% of salary.
- Salary: $70,000
- Your contribution: $2,100 (3% of salary)
- Employer adds: $2,100
- Total invested: $4,200 — a **100% immediate return** on your contribution

**Always contribute at least enough to get the full employer match.** Not doing so is leaving part of your compensation on the table.

## Traditional vs. Roth 401(k)

| Feature | Traditional 401(k) | Roth 401(k) |
|---------|-------------------|-------------|
| Contributions | Pre-tax (reduces taxable income now) | After-tax (no immediate tax benefit) |
| Growth | Tax-deferred | Tax-free |
| Withdrawals | Taxed as ordinary income | Tax-free (if qualified) |
| Best if | You expect lower taxes in retirement | You expect higher taxes in retirement |
| RMDs | Required at age 73 | Required (unless rolled to Roth IRA) |

## Vesting

Employer contributions may have a **vesting schedule** — you must stay employed for a certain period before fully "owning" the match.

- **Cliff vesting**: 0% until 3 years, then 100%
- **Graded vesting**: 20% per year for 6 years

Your own contributions are always 100% vested immediately.

## Early Withdrawal Penalties

Withdrawing before age 59½ triggers:
- **10% early withdrawal penalty**
- **Ordinary income taxes on the amount withdrawn**

**Exceptions include:** Disability, death, medical expenses exceeding 7.5% of AGI, first-time home purchase (IRAs only), certain hardships.

## Investment Options

401(k) plans offer a menu of investment options — typically mutual funds. Key considerations:
- Look for low expense ratio index funds
- Avoid high-fee actively managed funds
- Consider target-date funds for simplicity

## Key Takeaways

1. Always contribute enough to capture the full employer match — it's free money
2. 2024 limit: $23,000 ($30,500 if 50+)
3. Traditional = tax break now; Roth = tax-free in retirement
4. Withdrawals before 59½ incur 10% penalty plus taxes
5. Choose low-cost index funds within your plan
