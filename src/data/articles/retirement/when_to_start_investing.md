## The Short Answer: Now

The single most important factor in retirement success is **time in the market**. Every year you delay costs significantly more than any other variable — more than your investment choices, more than your employer's match, more than stock selection.

## The Staggering Cost of Waiting

Assume a 7% average annual return (historical stock market average after inflation):

| Start Age | Monthly Investment | Total Invested | Balance at 65 |
|-----------|-------------------|----------------|---------------|
| 25 | $500 | $240,000 (40 yrs) | **$1,312,000** |
| 30 | $500 | $210,000 (35 yrs) | **$924,000** |
| 35 | $500 | $180,000 (30 yrs) | **$643,000** |
| 40 | $500 | $150,000 (25 yrs) | **$438,000** |
| 45 | $500 | $120,000 (20 yrs) | **$289,000** |

Starting at 25 vs. 35 — the same $500/month — produces **$669,000 more** at retirement. That 10-year head start is worth more than the total contributions made after 35.

## The "Wait Until I Make More Money" Trap

Many people delay investing, telling themselves they'll start when:
- They earn more
- They pay off student loans
- They buy a house
- They have a family

Each delay compounds the cost. The "right time" to start is always earlier than you think.

**Even small amounts matter enormously:**
- $50/month from age 22 to 65 at 7% = **$175,000**
- $0/month from 22 to 32, then $100/month from 32 to 65 = **$133,000**

The person who invested half as much, for longer, ends up with more.

## The Priority Order for Young Investors

1. **Capture employer 401(k) match** — Always first. It's a 50–100% instant return.
2. **Build emergency fund** ($1,000 starter, then 3–6 months)
3. **Roth IRA**: $7,000/year max. Tax-free growth is most valuable when young.
4. **Maximize 401(k)**: Up to $23,000/year
5. **Taxable investing**: No limits, after maxing tax-advantaged accounts

## Why the Roth IRA is Perfect for Young Investors

Young people are typically in lower tax brackets than they'll be in peak earning years. A Roth IRA lets you:
- Pay taxes now at your current lower rate
- Grow the investment completely tax-free
- Withdraw in retirement with zero tax

The tax-free compounding over 30–40 years is extraordinary. $1,000 in a Roth at age 25, growing at 7% for 40 years, becomes **$14,974** — completely tax-free.

## Common Obstacles and Solutions

**"I have student loan debt"**
For loans above ~6-7% interest, prioritize payoff over investing (beyond the employer match). Below that rate, consider investing and paying minimum loan payments.

**"I can't afford to invest"**
Start with as little as $25–$50/month. Automate it so you don't miss the money. Increase by 1% each year.

**"The market might crash"**
Yes, it might. But you're investing for decades — short-term crashes are buying opportunities. Every historical bear market eventually recovered.

**"I don't know how to invest"**
Choose a target-date fund (e.g., "Target Date 2060 Fund") in your 401(k). It automatically manages allocation and gradually becomes more conservative as you approach retirement. Simple and effective.

## How Much Should You Save?

A common target: **15% of gross income** toward retirement (including employer match).

If 15% feels impossible, start with whatever you can and increase by 1% each year (coincide with raises).

## Key Takeaways

1. Start investing as early as possible — time is the most powerful factor
2. Even small amounts early beat large amounts later
3. Always capture the full employer 401(k) match first
4. Roth IRA is particularly powerful for young, lower-income investors
5. If unsure where to start, use a target-date fund in your 401(k)
